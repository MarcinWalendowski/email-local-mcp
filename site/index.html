Why it is safe

The honest answer, in four parts

Handing an agent your email is a real decision. These are the specific reasons it is a defensible one, rather than a reassurance that it is.

01 · Nothing is uploaded

Your mail never leaves your machine

There is no server run by this project, no account, and no analytics or telemetry of any kind. The engine opens a direct connection to your mail provider, exactly as any desktop mail client does. The only parties are you, your computer, and your provider.

Precisely: the macOS app makes one other outbound request, an update check against raw.githubusercontent.com. It sends nothing about you or your mail. The command line tool makes none at all.

Your agent does see the messages it is asked to read. That is the point, and it happens on whatever terms you already have with it.

02 · Revocable credentials

Your password is not the credential

You never hand over your real password. You create an App Password, or sign in with OAuth: a per-app credential you can revoke at any moment without touching your account.

It is stored only in your operating system's credential store. Never in a config file, never in the repository, never in a log line, and never in a response your agent can read back.

03 · Locked to the loopback

Nothing outside can reach the server

A local port that speaks to your mailbox is worth attacking, so it is closed three ways at once: bound to 127.0.0.1 and unreachable from your network, a bearer token on every request, and Origin validation.

That last one is what stops a malicious page in your browser quietly talking to the port behind your back, an attack called DNS rebinding.

04 · Deletion is gated

The irreversible things ask first

Trash is the default and it is reversible. Permanent deletion is a separate tool that refuses to run without confirm:true.

Bulk operations preview with dryRun:true, changing nothing. Anything destructive, or any batch over 100 messages, needs explicit confirmation. An account added --read-only refuses every write, whatever the agent decides to try.

What you ask for

Ordinary sentences, across every inbox

Most people live in several mailboxes: personal, work, a side project, an old address that still gets the important things. Every request below spans all of them at once.

YouWhat did I miss this week across all my accounts?
YouFind the invoice from Acme, any mailbox, and save the PDF to my desktop.
YouReply to Sarah's last email agreeing to Tuesday, but keep it short.
YouUnsubscribe sweep: trash every promotional email older than a year.
YouMove everything from my accountant into a Tax folder.

The last two touch thousands of messages and are still one request. The bulk tools act on a whole search result in a single pass instead of one call per message, so a ten thousand message cleanup never trips your agent's tool timeout.

How it works

One engine, one provider per account

Every account speaks IMAP and SMTP, so search, read, send, draft, move, archive, trash and attachments work everywhere. What differs is what the protocol itself exposes.

Before you install

What it does not do yet

A page that only lists strengths is not much use for deciding. These are the real edges.

It is pre-1.0. It works and is in daily use, but tool names and options may still change between versions.

The macOS app is not notarized yet. The first launch is blocked and needs "Open Anyway" in System Settings. A Developer ID release will end that.

Non-Gmail search is weaker. IMAP offers a server-side text match, not Gmail's operators. Mapping the common ones onto IMAP SEARCH is the next thing on the roadmap.

OAuth needs a client you register. Mail scopes are restricted, so a client id shipped inside a public binary would force this project through annual security assessment, and would expire your tokens weekly until it passed. Registering your own takes about five minutes.

Linux needs a running Secret Service. gnome-keyring or KWallet, with an unlocked login keyring.
